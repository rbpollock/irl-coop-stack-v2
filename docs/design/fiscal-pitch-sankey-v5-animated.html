The same dollar, two stacks

A small community organization that gathers donations — say, feeding people in urban centers — and how it spends a whole dollar. Flip the toggle; watch the middle move.

One dollar, four honestly-split destinations for a small donation-funded org: reaches a person · staff & delivery · tools & platforms · recirculates in the community. Figures are derived estimates (bottom: the sector "~75-90% program" counts staff as program; the only hard sourced anchors are cash-aid ~87¢ and food-aid ~45¢ reaching the person, per GiveDirectly and the UN WFP). Rented stack sums to $100; shared node re-spends the same $100.

funding in reaches a person staff & delivery tools & platforms (subscriptions · fees · processors) recirculates in the community
The honest dollar: a small community org feeds about 20¢, pays people 30¢ to do the work, and hands ~40¢ to the tools & platforms that connect and record it — subscriptions, per-seat fees, processors, platform takes. The community itself keeps roughly nothing. The only hard fact in that run: for a staffed delivery org, the sector's "75–90% to program" is dominated by that very staff — the person eats a small share of it. On a shared node the ~40 the tools and landlords took goes to ~18, and the released ~30 stays in the community. Fewer corporate fingers, more of the same dollar.
Honesty — fix before ships: figures are derived, not measured. The person-reaches anchor is real-sourced only in the extreme (GiveDirectly ~87¢ cash; UN WFP ~45¢ food); in a community delivery org the real number is lower-bounded by staff/tools share and estimated here as ~20¢. "Recirculates → 30" on the shared side is the platform take that stops leaving; it's a designed property, not yet a banked figure. Replace all four with the coop's real scenario numbers before this goes anywhere near a sponsor. The "recipient becomes a member" loop is designed, not building.
