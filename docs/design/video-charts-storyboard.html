Five charts carry the whole thing

The cost model holds ~50 numbers. About six belong on screen. Everything else is ammunition for the Q&A, not the video — which is the real answer to "it's a lot of information".

Chart discipline for these videos
  • One idea per chart. If a chart needs a paragraph, it's two charts or zero.
  • Eight to twelve seconds. Then cut to the next one. Never narrate a chart while it animates.
  • Animate the divergence, not the numbers. Let the lines pull apart on screen; the audience reads the gap, not the axis.
  • Seven elements maximum. No pie charts, no 3-D, no dual axes, no unlabelled log scales.
  • Every figure date-stamped on screen. List prices, 2026-09-13, source named in the corner.
  • Numbers are the receipt, not the plot. A viewer should be able to describe the chart from memory an hour later — "the flat line versus the steep one".

What stays out of the videos entirely

  • The 48-row comparable table. It exists so that one number can be quoted with a source — not to be read.
  • The tier definitions (lean/standard/enterprise) and the seat-scoping argument. Both are disclosures for the doc and the Q&A.
  • Every "we could be wrong here" caveat. Ten of them live in the model; a video says the two that matter for that video, out loud, before the audience does.
  • The storage measurement detail (du output, the duplicate basemap, the unreadable postgres dir). Keep the number and the fact that it was measured; the method is the receipts.
  • Charts of numbers nobody will remember. If a figure can't be said in one spoken sentence, it's a caption, not a chart.
