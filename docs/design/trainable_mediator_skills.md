# Weaved Framework: Trainable Mediator Skills for Real-Time Cooperation Intervention

## Synthesis: Four Threads Woven Into One

This document weaves together four research strands into a single, operable framework for human intervention mediators working in digital groups:

1. **Ethnomethodology / Conversation Analysis** (Duneier & Molotch): the sequential machinery of interaction — turn-taking, repair, adjacency pairs, transition-relevance places
2. **Hidden Information Labs** (Matziorinis): network topology, social ripple behaviors, coercive attractors, entropy dynamics
3. **Public-Health Violence Interruption** (Cure Violence): real-time detection, credibility-based intervention, structural cooling, relapse prevention
4. **Pathways Cooperative Governance**: MutualRespectEdge, transparent attribution, spec-anchored intervention auditing

The result is not a general theory but a **trainable skill taxonomy**: specific perceptual, cognitive, and performative abilities that human mediators can develop, with explicit training pathways, practice protocols, and calibration checks.

---

## Core Principle: Topology Before Personality

Every skill in this framework rests on the HILI principle: **the question is never who someone is, but where they stand in the interactional topology.** The mediator's job is to read structure, diagnose contaminations, inject entropy where attractors have formed, and preserve or restore reciprocal edges.

Personality judgments ("they're a troll," "they're toxic") are epistemically lazy and practically counterproductive. They trigger narrative entrenchment and reduce the system's degrees of freedom. The trained mediator suspends personality attribution and operates entirely on structural readings until the topology has shifted.

## Skill Taxonomy for Human Mediators

### Domain 1: Topological Reading

**Skill 1.1 — Map the Interactional Graph**
*Read a conversation thread as a network, not as a sequence of opinions.*

Trainable practices:
- **Node audit:** Who is present? Who is silent? Whose presence is felt even when they haven't spoken? (Ghost participants with unexpressed stakes)
- **Edge directionality:** Map each turn as a directed edge. Are edges bidirectional (A speaks, B responds, A acknowledges) or unilateral (A speaks, B is ignored, A speaks again)?
- **Centrality drift:** Track whose nodes accumulate the highest in-degree (being responded to) and out-degree (initiating). Healthy threads have rotating centrality; unhealthy threads have fixed star topology.
- **Structural holes:** Identify where information should flow but doesn't. Is there a pair of participants who hold complementary knowledge but have no direct adjacency pair between them?

Training protocol:
- Take anonymized chat logs (identity hidden, content visible). Draw the interaction graph by hand. Compare with an EMCA coding of the same thread.
- Drill: "In this thread, who is structurally isolated even though they spoke three times?"
- Calibration: Compare hand-drawn maps against algorithmic sentiment/centrality output. Discrepancies reveal mediator bias (e.g., privileging content over structure).

**Skill 1.2 — Detect Coercive Attractors**
*Recognize when a thread is falling into a low-entropy, self-sustaining conflict pattern regardless of the topic being discussed.*

Trainable practices:
- **Pattern lock detection:** Are the same two participants cycling through the same adjacency-pair types (challenge → rechallenge → counterchallenge) without variation or repair?
- **Closure refusal:** Does the thread resist every attempt at topic closure? Are transition-relevance places systematically ignored or re-opened?
- **Narrowing vocabulary:** Is the vocabulary of the thread collapsing into a small set of charged terms, with decreasing alignment markers (acknowledgments, concessions, confirmations)?
- **Latency polarization:** Are response latencies bifurcating into very fast (reactive, unconsidered) and very slow (strategic withholding)?

Training protocol:
- Annotated corpus method: Review threads that ended in flameouts, bans, or group exits. Work backward to identify when the attractor formed. The goal is to find the **tipping point**, not the trigger.
- Drill: Given a 50-turn thread, mark the turn where the system lost two or more degrees of topological freedom.
- Calibration: Compare human-marked tipping points against automated latent-heat indicators (sudden drops in repair success rate). High agreement = trained capacity; systematic human lateness = overcautious bias.

### Domain 2: Sequential Listening

**Skill 2.1 — Hear the Repair Machinery**
*Perceive whether a thread is self-correcting or self-corroding by listening to repair sequences, not semantic content.*

Trainable practices:
- **Repair initiation types:** Distinguish self-repair (speaker corrects themselves), other-initiated repair (listener requests clarification), and other-repair (listener corrects speaker). Track the ratio.
- **Repair success rate:** Does the repair sequence close? Or does it spiral into meta-dispute about the repair itself ("I didn't mean it that way" / "Then why did you say it?")?
- **Embedded vs exposed repair:** Is repair handled inline (brief, cooperative) or does it require meta-commentary that derails the topic?

Training protocol:
- Audio training with transcripts: Listen to recorded meetings while following the transcript. Mark every repair initiation. Check against EMCA-coded gold standard.
- Drill: Given a thread with 15 repair initiations, classify each as successful/failed and predict whether the thread will resolve or escalate. Test against behavioral aftermath (did participants return?).
- Note: This skill is harder in text than speech because text lacks prosody. The mediator must compensate by reading punctuation, editing traces, and response latency as surrogate prosody.

**Skill 2.2 — Identify Interactional Vandalism**
*Recognize systematic disruptions to the practical ethics of conversation — patterns that make cooperation structurally impossible without appearing to violate explicit rules.*

Trainable practices:
- **Withholding recognition:** A participant makes a proposal, offers a repair, or invites agreement. The response is non-acknowledgment (topic shift, silence, or an unrelated turn). Track how often this happens to specific participants.
- **Strategic ambiguity:** A turn is constructed to be simultaneously challengeable and deniable ("just asking questions" topology). The mediator learns to feel when a turn is designed to maximize the other participant's decision burden.
- **Flood-and-drown:** Rapid-fire messages that exhaust others' capacity to respond, creating the appearance of a monologue thread even when others are nominally present.
- **Topic hijacking:** A transition-relevance place that should close topic A is used to launch topic B, erasing A's pending status.

Training protocol:
- Duneier/Molotch case study: Walk through the sidewalk transcripts. Identify where the men's interactional vandalism operates. Then map equivalent patterns in digital text.
- Drill: Given a thread with one flagged participant, identify which of four patterns above is operating without reading the semantic content. (Test: semantic content is scrambled, grammatical structure preserved. Can the mediator still identify the pattern?)
- Calibration: Compare mediator flags against group member complaints. Mediator should flag interactional vandalism *before* complaints crystallize, not after.

### Domain 3: Intervention Design

**Skill 3.1 — Inject Entropy Without Extinguishing**
*Introduce structural perturbations that break coercive attractors without shaming participants or destroying the relationship substrate.*

Trainable practices:
- **Temporal cooling:** Introduce a mandatory pause, branching requirement, or asynchronous period. The key skill is **calibrating the duration and framing** — long enough to break the attractor, short enough to prevent narrative solidification ("they shut me down").
- **Spatial restructuring:** Force threading, introduce a third-party bridging node, or split a dyadic conflict into parallel sub-threads. The mediator must choose the topology that isolates the attractor without isolating the people.
- **Register shifting:** Move the thread from a high-arousal channel (synchronous chat) to a low-arousal channel (async document). Or shift from text to voice. This changes the interactional physics without addressing content.
- **Procedural reframing:** Recharacterize the thread as a process with explicit stages ("Let's take proposals first, then concerns"). This restructures adjacency-pair expectations.

Training protocol:
- Simulation with confederates: Run a thread that naturally spirals. Trainee mediator intervenes at a pre-specified turn with a chosen entropy injection. Measure: does the thread return to reciprocal basin? Do participants report feeling heard? Does interactional vocabulary broaden afterward?
- Calibration: Over-intervention (extinguishes the thread, no one returns) vs under-intervention (attractor reforms within 5 turns). The target range is 2–4 turns spent in procedural repair, then return to topic.

**Skill 3.2 — Reciprocal Edge Restoration**
*Rebuild bidirectional information flow between participants who have lost mutual information.*

Trainable practices:
- **Uptake amplification:** Deliberately surface and validate moments of alignment ("A proposed X, B refined it—this is the kind of cooperative thread we need"). Make reciprocity visible to the participants themselves.
- **Repair coaching:** When one participant attempts repair and it fails, the mediator privately coaches them on the structural reason ("Your repair was a self-justification rather than an acknowledgment of B's turn. Try again, leading with B's frame.")
- **Bridge construction:** Identify what each participant actually needs from the other (not positionally, but informationally). Restate each side in the other's vocabulary. This is not compromise — it is **translation at the edge**.
- **Reciprocity ritualization:** Introduce lightweight, explicit reciprocity formats ("Before responding to objections, name one thing you took from the previous speaker's turn").

Training protocol:
- Paired roleplay: Two trainees enact a recurring conflict from their own experience. Third trainee mediates using only reciprocal-edge techniques. Debrief: did mutual information increase? Measured by post-mediation vocabulary-alignment score.
- Calibration: Track whether restored edges persist without mediator presence. Success = edge holds for 48+ hours post-intervention.

**Skill 3.3 — Alignment Recentering**
*Zoom out from tactical dispute to restate the highest-level shared goals, so participants remember they are aligned on destination before fighting about path.*

Trainable practices:
- **Goal archaeology:** Before intervening, identify the most abstract goal that all parties still claim to share. This is often two levels above the current dispute. State it explicitly.
- **Alignment restatement:** Frame the intervention by anchoring to that shared goal: "It seems like everyone here wants [X]. The disagreement is about how to get there. Can we confirm X is still the shared destination?"
- **Pathway pluralism:** Once alignment is restated, make visible that multiple pathways can serve the same goal. "If the goal is X, A proposes route 1, B proposes route 2. Are there conditions under which either route would work?"
- **Cost-of-pathway comparison:** Shift the frame from "which path is right?" to "what does each path cost, and who pays?"

Training protocol:
- Take 10 threads where participants eventually discovered they agreed on goals but fought on means. Practice identifying the alignment level within the first 5 turns. Compare with actual resolution.
- Drill: Given a thread in pure tactical combat (feature A vs feature B), produce three different alignment statements at escalating goal abstraction. Test which one stops the spiraling.

**Skill 3.4 — Stratify the Disagreement**
*Separate strategic disagreement (what world are we building?) from tactical disagreement (which button do we click?), and prevent tactical fights from masquerading as existential divides.*

Trainable practices:
- **Disagreement taxonomy mapping:** Ask participants to classify each point of dispute as Strategic, Tactical, or Empirical. A Strategic dispute is irreconcilable if true (competing north stars). A Tactical dispute is reconcilable by experiment (try A, then B). An Empirical dispute is reconcilable by evidence (we don't yet know which works).
- **Escalation block:** When a tactical dispute is being framed as strategic ("If we do X, we've abandoned our principles"), reframe: "Is X actually a principle, or is it a mechanism?"
- **Isolation routing:** Route strategic, tactical, and empirical disputes into different threads or sessions. Strategic disputes need the full group with governance authority. Tactical disputes need a small experiment team. Empirical disputes need a research brief.

Training protocol:
- Annotate 20 resolved disputes. Tag each turn with its actual disagreement type and its claimed type. Identify the turns where mismatched framing drove escalation.
- Simulation: Confederates escalate a tactical dispute into a strategic one. Trainee mediator de-escalates by stratifying. Measure: does the group adopt the taxonomy?

**Skill 3.5 — Hypothesis Escalation and Epistemic Calibration**
*When participants argue a hypothetical indefinitely, intervene by making explicit what empirical condition would increase confidence and who is responsible for generating it.*

Trainable practices:
- **Confidence audit:** Ask each side: "What fact, if known, would move your confidence above 80%?" If no fact is named, the dispute is aesthetic/vibes, not empirical.
- **Research assignment:** Convert the hypothetical into a testable claim with an evidence standard. "You believe X will hurt adoption. What adoption metric, measured over what period, would prove or disprove this?"
- **Decision staging:** If evidence is impossible or too slow, stage the decision: "We cannot resolve this empirically in time. Can we agree on a 30-day trial with a kill condition?"
- **Vibes naming:** When a position is genuinely vibes-based ("I just feel this is wrong"), name it explicitly without shame: "This is a taste/values call, not a prediction. Taste calls are valid but need different resolution mechanisms than empirical ones."

Training protocol:
- Drill: Given a thread stuck on a hypothetical ("What if users hate it?"), produce three different research assignments that would resolve the uncertainty. Check: are they falsifiable? Are they feasible?
- Calibration: Track whether research-assigned disputes resolve faster than unassigned ones. Target: median time-to-resolution drops by >50%.

**Skill 3.6 — Analogical and Mythopoetic Resourcing**
*Use analogy, myth, historical parable, or narrative archetype to reframe a stuck position without attacking identity or requiring immediate concession.*

Trainable practices:
- **Analogical mapping:** Identify a structurally analogous situation from history, myth, or another domain that mirrors the current dynamic but is emotionally neutral to the participants. "This pattern reminds me of the [X] negotiations, where both sides wanted alliance but couldn't agree on tribute order."
- **Mythopoetic distancing:** Use archetypal language to depersonalize the conflict. "We seem to be replaying the Tower of Babel moment — same goal, different vocabulary, mutual incomprehension."
- **Historical context injection:** Draw on the group's own records or established shared history. "In the 2024 refactor debate, we had the same split. We tried A, then B. What did we learn about which conditions make A work?"
- **Genre shifting:** Reframe the thread's genre. "Right now this reads as a courtroom drama. What if we rewrote it as an expedition planning session? Who brings the map, who brings the rations?"

Training protocol:
- Build a personal analogy library: 20 stories/analogies that map to common interactional attractors (dyadic lock, flood-and-drown, repair refusal, etc.). Practice deploying them until they feel natural.
- Peer review: After using an analogy, ask participants whether it clarified or confused. Analogy failure usually means the mapping was too forced or culturally distant.
- Calibration: Track whether threads where analogy was used return to reciprocal basin faster than threads with direct confrontation.

**Skill 3.7 — Positional Exchange (Steel Intervention)**
*Require each participant to articulate the other's position in terms the other would accept, before proceeding with their own rebuttal.*

Trainable practices:
- **Mandatory steel-man:** Before responding to an objection, each participant must restate the previous position to the speaker's satisfaction: "I want to make sure I'm representing you fairly. You're saying [X]. Is that accurate?"
- **Role-reversal thread:** Create a parallel thread where each participant argues the other's position for 3 turns. This is not mockery — it is enforced perspective-taking.
- **Position swap documentation:** After the exchange, each participant edits a shared document stating the other's strongest argument in their own words. The document becomes the ground for further discussion.
- **Empirical prediction convergence:** After positional exchange, identify what both sides predict will happen under the other's proposal. Often the predictions converge, collapsing the strategic disagreement.

Training protocol:
- Ideological Turing test drill: Participants write a defense of a position they oppose. Another participant (who holds that position) grades it for accuracy and fairness. Target: 90% acceptance rate.
- Calibration: Measure whether positional exchange reduces defensive latency in subsequent turns. Success = average response latency drops by >30% after exchange.

**Skill 3.8 — Record-Based Grounding**
*Use the group's own documented history to constrain hypothetical spirals and anchor abstract disputes in concrete precedent.*

Trainable practices:
- **Precedent retrieval:** Before a tactical dispute escalates, surface a relevant prior decision or experiment from the group's records. "We faced a similar tradeoff in [prior thread]. We chose X under conditions Y. Are conditions Y present here?"
- **Decision archaeology:** When participants disagree about what happened in a prior case, use the actual records (chat logs, pathway runs, decision receipts) rather than memory. Human memory of conflict is systematically distorted.
- **Analogous-outcome projection:** Use historical records to project outcomes. "Last time we argued this hypothetical for 3 weeks, then chose A. The outcome was Z. Does that change our confidence in either pathway?"
- **Pattern documentation:** When a new attractor type emerges, document it explicitly for future mediators. Build a group-specific taxonomy of sabotage and resolution patterns.

Training protocol:
- Archive literacy drill: Given a current dispute, find the closest historical analogue in the group's records within 10 minutes. Practice search strategies (by date, by participants, by topic cluster).
- Calibration: Interventions that cite records vs interventions that don't. Compare resolution rates and relapse rates. Target: record-cited interventions have lower relapse.

### Domain 4: Contagion Containment

**Skill 4.1 — Map Ripple Propagation**
*Track how interactional sabotage in one thread degrades adjacent edges and poisons group priors.*

Trainable practices:
- **Cross-thread spillover detection:** A participant who was burned in Thread A enters Thread B with elevated defensive latency. Detect the behavioral signature.
- **Observer effect tracking:** Silent participants in Thread A update their interactional strategy for Thread C based on what they observed. The mediator reads this in C before it becomes explicit.
- **Narrative crystallization:** A local conflict in Thread A becomes shorthand for group identity ("the time X happened"). The mediator tracks when the narrative achieves sufficient social weight that it becomes self-fulfilling.

Training protocol:
- Multi-thread simulation: Run three simultaneous threads with shared participants. Introduce sabotage in Thread A. Trainee mediator predicts which participants in Threads B and C will show spillover within N turns. Compare against behavioral data.

**Skill 4.2 — Quarantine Without Isolation**
*Temporarily contain a contaminated subnetwork while preserving the possibility of reintegration.*

Trainable practices:
- **Thread-level cooling:** Apply entropy injection to the contaminated thread only, without group-wide announcements that would amplify the narrative.
- **Edge-level filtering:** If one participant's turns are structurally destructive when directed at a specific target but productive elsewhere, route their contributions through a structured layer (e.g., require async-first posting on topics involving that target) rather than group-level exclusion.
- **Precedent management:** Ensure that containment decisions are documented in structural terms ("thread showed coercive attractor signature") rather than identity terms ("X is difficult"), so return is possible without narrative rewriting.

Training protocol:
- Case study archive: Review containment decisions that succeeded (participant returned to reciprocal basin) vs failed (participant left or permanarrative formed). Identify structural differences in how containment was framed.

### Domain 5: Playback Facilitation

**Skill 5.1 — Translate Topology Into Felt Experience**
*Use real-time data visualizations to help participants perceive interactional dynamics that they are otherwise blind to.*

Trainable practices:
- **Heat-strip narration:** Walk participants through a turn-density or latency heat strip in real time. "Notice this 10-minute block: four participants, but 80% of edges originate from one node. How does that feel from your positions?"
- **Repair-chain excavation:** Display a repair chain that spiraled. Ask each participant to mark where they felt the thread shift from cooperative to stuck. Compare their felt moments against structural indicators.
- **Alignment trajectory projection:** Show a thread's lexical-alignment score over time. "Here you were converging. Here you diverged. What happened at this timestamp?"

Training protocol:
- Visualization literacy course: Mediators must be fluent in reading the automated outputs before they can facilitate others' reading of them.
- Calibration check: When mediators narrate a heat strip, do participants' subsequent self-reports match the structural reading? Mismatches reveal where the mediator is imposing narrative rather than surfacing structure.

**Skill 5.2 — Co-Create Intervention Ownership**
*Ensure that structural interventions feel like group decisions, not moderator impositions.*

Trainable practices:
- **Transparent thresholding:** Show participants the exact indicators and thresholds that triggered the intervention. "The system flagged this thread when repair success rate dropped below 40% for 6 consecutive turns. Do you agree this matches your experience?"
- **Option presentation, not verdict:** Present two or three entropy-injection options. Let the thread vote or consent.
- **Predictive framing:** Before intervening, predict what would happen without intervention. Afterward, review whether the prediction held. This builds shared epistemic trust in the system.

Training protocol:
- Recorded facilitation review: Review video of trainee-led playback sessions. Code for moments where the mediator shifted from structural narration to implicit judgment. Train away from judgment.

### Domain 6: Self-Calibration

**Skill 6.1 — Detect Your Own Bias Drift**
*Recognize when your structural readings are being contaminated by your own relational history, cultural frame, or emotional state.*

Trainable practices:
- **Demographic mismatch audit:** If your training corpus is predominantly standard-American-professional, your contempt detector will misfire on AAE prosody, neurodivergent pacing, or non-Western adjacency norms. Build explicit cross-cultural calibration into your practice.
- **Emotional state logging:** Before each mediation session, self-rate arousal and valence. After, compare your intervention decisions against a blind structural analysis. Did your state shift the threshold?
- **Over-intervention tracking:** Count how many interventions you performed this week. If the count trends up while conflict severity is flat, you are likely performing anxiety regulation, not structural repair.

Training protocol:
- Monthly calibration session: Review your last 10 interventions with a peer or algorithmic mirror. Did you intervene earlier/harsher on participants whose interactional style differs from yours? Did you ignore attractors that involved people you like?

**Skill 6.2 — Maintain Reciprocal Edges to the System**
*The mediator must remain in a reciprocal relationship with the group, not become a surveillance authority.*

Trainable practices:
- **Accountability thread:** The mediator's own decisions are subject to the same structural analysis as any other thread. Their interventions are logged, receipted, and reviewable.
- **Consensual mandate:** Mediation authority is renewed through periodic group consent, not assumed by role installation.
- **Vulnerability signaling:** When the mediator makes a misreading, they repair it publicly. This models that repair is normal and non-shaming.

## Skill Progression Pathway

| Phase | Duration | Focus | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observer** | 2–4 weeks | Topological reading, sequential listening | Annotate 10 threads; achieve 80% agreement with gold-standard EMCA coding |
| **Apprentice** | 4–8 weeks | Detection + playback facilitation | Lead 5 playback sessions; participant self-report alignment with structural reading |
| **Practitioner** | 3–6 months | Entropy injection + reciprocal edge restoration | 10 interventions with >60% return-to-reciprocal-basin rate within 48 hours |
| **Senior** | 12+ months | Contagion containment + quarantine + system governance | Design intervention protocols adopted by group; train Observer-level mediators |

## Central Claim

> Human mediators in digital groups can be systematically trained to function as cooperation interrupters — not by developing intuition or charisma, but by acquiring **structural literacy**: the ability to read interactional topology, detect coercive attractors, inject calibrated entropy, restore reciprocal edges, and contain social-ripple contagion before it rewires the group's network architecture. These skills are trainable, assessable, and auditable. When combined with real-time automated detection and transparent Pathways governance, they create a cooperative immune system: human credibility plus structural visibility plus consensual intervention.
