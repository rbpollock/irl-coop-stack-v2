**Subject:** The layer under your list — co-authoring it, not pitching you

Hi Jessica,

I've read the 300+ list twice. The line I keep returning to is the one you close on: the problem
was never the ideas, it was that nobody could see they were connected.

I think that's half of it. The other half doesn't go away once everyone can see each other. Two
movements can find each other, agree completely, and still have no way to act together. They can't
hold money jointly, share a member list, or run a project on the same surface. Each group rents its
tools separately and owns none of them. That's why your list can have cooperatives at the top and,
three hundred entries later, still nothing on it that lets two groups operate as one thing.

Your site has a Builders track: people already running something local who need it seen. Visibility
is the whole of that track, and you're right that it's missing. What's also missing is that two of
those builders, three blocks apart, can both be seen and still have no way to build one thing
together.

I've been building the layer that's missing. It's called irl.coop.

The short version: the group is the account. Not a login with a shared password — a Safe the group
controls, with seats, roles and votes, and access that's scoped per member and can be taken back.
One identity across every tool instead of a new account per app. Chat, documents, projects, events
with public ticketing, mail on the group's own domain, maps, and search across the group's own
knowledge. All of it on infrastructure the group runs, so the cost doesn't grow every time someone
joins.

Federation is the part that matters for your list. Groups keep their own identity, rules and money.
Nothing has to be agreed beyond the ground underneath. You wrote that diversity in thinking is a
feature, not a problem to resolve. That's the design constraint here, not a compromise. No central
HQ is the requirement.

Where it actually stands, so you're not guessing: one node, running, used in development. Identity,
groups and roles, scoped access, documents, projects, events, mail, maps and search are built. The
shared treasury and the interconnection between separate groups are designed, not built. Nothing has
been demonstrated inside a real group's daily work yet. We publish that — every user-visible claim on
the site is classified as shipped, designed or aspirational, and a maturity table records the gaps.

**What I'm asking**

Not money, and not an endorsement.

I'd like you to co-author it. You've spent four years documenting what these movements actually need.
I've been building what one platform thinks they need. Those should be the same piece of work, and
right now they aren't, which is my fault as much as anyone's.

Three concrete forms. Take any, or none.

1. **Steward it with us.** Take a seat in the group that governs the stack. Not an advisory title —
   real decisions about what gets built and what gets refused.
2. **Run Connectioning on it.** Your coordination layer is itself a group with members, money and
   records. It could be one of the first real groups on the stack, with you holding your group's
   account instead of renting one. I'll do the work of getting it there.
3. **Open a door.** Pick three movements from your list that keep hitting this wall, and I'll put a
   working stack in front of them. We publish what breaks. If it breaks, that's a finding, and you're
   the person who gets to report it.

The honest version: the stack is early, and the movements are the authority on what it should be. You
are the best-placed person in this movement to tell me where it's wrong.

I also made a short film of the argument — thirty seconds, the map and the road under it. It's yours
to use wherever it's useful, including in your own writing, no strings.

Worth thirty minutes?

— Robbie
irl.coop
