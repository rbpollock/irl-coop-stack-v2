## How the pronunciation is handled

NeuTTS reads "irl.coop" as two words ("earl" + "coop"). To force the right
pronunciation the on-screen/script text spells it phonetically:

- **"I.R.L."** — periods between letters, so it reads the initials, not "earl".
- **"co-op"** — hyphen, so it reads as "co-op", not "coop" (as in chicken coop).

Every place irl.coop is spoken, the narration says **"I.R.L. co-op"** with those
markers. Confirmed by the phonetic spelling, not left to the TTS's default.

## The humanized script

(Replaces the current staccato version. Real sentences, second person, no

### 1 · Title  (≈10s)
**Old:** "Goodbye Group Gouging. The tools a group needs to cooperate have a hidden price — and it's not what you think."
**New:** If you're thinking about creating or running a group, the tools you need to cooperate with each other come with a massive price tag... so most groups sell out or hardly use tech tools."

### 2 · Divergence  (≈11s)
**Old:** "Watch these two lines. The blue one is what a group pays on a normal stack. The flat one is the same work on one shared node. That gap is the price of coordination."
**New:** Watch these two lines for a second. The steep one is what your group shells out when you rent your tools one by one. The flat one is the same work on a cooperative platform. And the distance between them, that gap, is why most groups never try to scale or interconnect.

### 3 · Wall  (≈13s)
**Old:** "And what's the catch. It isn't a price you can shop. Business tools cap at three hundred users. The self service menu ends before five hundred people..."
**New:** Most of these tools just stop listing their prices at a few hundred people, and getting past that means talking to sales and signing a contract you can't walk away from.  When you need big money investors just for software subscriptions it squashes the garden club, the neighborhood solar project, the farm collective before they even start.

### 4 · Three ways  (≈17s)
**Old:** "So at forty members, you have three ways to buy the same capability..."
**New:** So at forty members there's a few ways to buy the same group of tools. Pay per person, which is nine or more separate subscriptions. Or you bundle it into those platforms that charge one fee but make growing unaffordable. Or you choose to OWN your own platform collectively, a co-op that hands control back to its users with transparent pricing and the lowest possible cost.

### 5 · Per participant  (≈11s)
**Old:** "Per person, that's three hundred and fifty dollars a year commercial, against two dollars and change on the node."
**New:** Per person, on most of these subscription platforms, that works out to a few hundred dollars a year for each of your group members, versus a couple of dollars when everyone shares.

### 6 · Redundancy  (≈11s)
**Old:** "Redundancy costs pennies when you own the hardware. Seventy cents a month for three copies. On the cloud it's nine times that."
**New:** Here's the part most people don't care about, but when we share the platform, we do. To keep a group's stuff safe. We need duplicate copies, encrypted, stored around the world. And we need that without data centers. On the cloud it's way more expensive to your pocket and the environment, and an open book for hostile adversaries to either lock you out or shut you down.

### 7 · Crossover  (≈11s)
**Old:** "Here's the self-sustaining line. The node's cost is fixed. Each group contributes a little. At thirteen groups it pays for itself."
**New:** Here's the way the math works with a secure, shared platform. One machine running the data storage and software is a fixed cost, and you split it among the groups that use it. At about a dozen groups it covers its own running. Everything a group adds after that is money the cooperative gets to distribute to support things we collectively care about.

### 8 · Value flow  (≈10s)
**Old:** "This is the difference. In a co-op, value circulates. In a platform, it climbs out."
**New:** The point of having a cooperative platform is to break the status quo. When the money stays inside your co-op, it circles back to the people who put it in. When you rent a platform, it climbs up and out to oligarchs. The question is just which direction you'd rather it went.

### 9 · Fear  (≈18s)
**Old:** "And when cooperation is costly, groups manage the cost by not writing things down..."
**New:** The only tools around all extract from a distance, and keep people from weaving in with the people around us. This structural and financial hurdle limits our imagination and keeps us apart. But where the local web actually grows — the trust, the talking, the safety, the support — it stops being a cheaper subscription and becomes something different. It lets a group carry capacity that none of them can hold alone: a harvest, a build, a shared thing done together, and only a cluster could carry it. And the price keeps falling, because there's no tether left to pay rent on. A company sends a bill. A web holds people. 

### 10 · Closing  (≈17s)
**Old:** "Cooperation is priced. A group shouldn't have to pay rent on its own tools."
**New:** So that's the whole pitch, real quick. Cooperation shouldn't have a rent you can't afford. At I.R.L. dot co-op, you own it all and set the terms. Right now it's an experiment: one node, your passion, revolutionary collaboration. Bring us a thing your group needs to get done this season, and we'll support you in getting it done. We can build the world we want to see in real life.
